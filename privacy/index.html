Privacy Policy.

PulseOS apps are designed to keep data usage minimal, clear, and local to your device.

Last updated: March 23, 2026

Overview

PulseOS respects your privacy. Our apps are built to provide useful functionality without unnecessary data collection, intrusive tracking, or advertising profiles.

What we collect

PulseOS apps are designed to work primarily on your device. Information you enter — such as settings, account values, notes, or preferences — stays local unless clearly stated otherwise.

We do not intentionally collect unnecessary personal information through our apps.

How data is used

App data is used only to provide the features of the app and support the experience you expect from the product.

We do not use your data to build marketing profiles or serve targeted advertising.

Analytics and tracking

PulseOS keeps analytics and tracking to a minimum. We do not use intrusive ad networks or external tracking tools to monitor your personal behavior across apps or websites.

Third parties

We do not sell your personal data to third parties.

If a PulseOS app uses third-party platform services such as Apple systems or app distribution tools, those services are governed by the privacy terms of the relevant platform provider.

Data security

We aim to keep app experiences simple and privacy-conscious. However, no device, software environment, or transmission method can be guaranteed to be completely secure.

Contact

Privacy-related questions? Reach out at support@pulseos.eu

App-specific

PulseVinyl

PulseVinyl checks your Apple Music library against vinyl release data and tracks what you own or want. Here's exactly what each service it relies on receives, why, and where that information lives.

Apple Music

Receives: read-only access to your Apple Music library (albums and artists), granted through Apple's MusicKit framework.

Why: to match your library against vinyl release data.

Stored: processed on your device; PulseOS does not receive your Apple ID or copy your library to its own servers. Governed by Apple's privacy policy.

iCloud / CloudKit

Receives: your owned and wanted records, notes, and other collection details.

Why: to sync your collection between your own devices.

Stored: in your private iCloud database via Apple's CloudKit — not on a PulseOS server, and not readable by us. Governed by Apple's privacy policy.

Discogs

Receives: the album title, artist name, barcode, or release ID needed to identify a record. If you optionally connect your own Discogs account, that connection is used for the same lookups — not to sync or upload your collection.

Why: to check vinyl availability, match barcodes, and estimate collection value from Discogs marketplace pricing.

Stored: an optional account's access token is stored securely in your iPhone's Keychain. Governed by Discogs' privacy policy.

MusicBrainz & Cover Art Archive

Receives: the same identifying details (barcode, title, artist) as a fallback when Discogs doesn't have a match.

Why: to fill in barcode metadata and album artwork.

Stored: not stored by PulseOS beyond the request. Governed by MusicBrainz's privacy policy.

ShazamKit

Receives: a short audio sample when you actively use PulseVinyl's music recognition — the microphone is only active during that recognition.

Why: to identify a song playing nearby.

Stored: PulseVinyl doesn't store the recording. Apple's ShazamKit framework converts it into a one-way acoustic signature that isn't shared back to Apple as audio. Governed by Apple's privacy policy.

Frankfurter

Receives: the currency codes needed for a conversion rate.

Why: to show estimated collection value in your local currency.

Stored: no collection contents, record details, or personal information are included in these requests.

PulseOS doesn't operate its own user-profile or analytics database for PulseVinyl, and we can't browse your music library or private collection — it lives in your Apple Music library and your own iCloud account. As with every PulseOS app, none of this data is sold or used for advertising. Third-party services above are governed by their own privacy policies, linked next to each one. Privacy questions?
